DispatchOS — Solo Operator transport preview.

DispatchOS is the transportation configuration of Solo Operator, a managed operations platform for a single operator running a real business. This is not a website. It is the working loop of the business — taking the booking, quoting it, confirming it, getting a driver on it, reminding the rider, and asking for the review afterwards.

What this is for

A single operator running transport work spends most of the admin day on the same handful of things: taking the booking, working out what to charge, remembering to remind the rider, telling a driver where to be, and chasing a review that never gets asked for. Each one is small. Together they are the job that stops the operator doing the job.

DispatchOS is aimed squarely at that loop and nothing else in the first version. The measure of whether it worked is not a feature count: does a booking get from request to completed trip to review request without the operator holding the whole thing in their head.

How the day runs now

  • Booking arrives by call or text and lives in someone's memory
  • Price worked out fresh each time, so two similar trips quote differently
  • Reminders happen when somebody remembers
  • Driver gets told by phone; status lives in the conversation
  • Reviews get asked for occasionally, or not at all
  • No-shows are found out about on arrival

How the day runs with DispatchOS

  • Booking is captured once and stays captured
  • Quote follows the operator's own rules, consistently
  • Confirmation and reminder go out on their own
  • Driver assignment and status are in one place
  • Review request fires when a trip is actually completed
  • Fewer missed pickups, standardised follow-up

SyMon AI for DispatchOS

Proposed for first version

SyMon AI is not a generic chatbot pasted onto a website. For DispatchOS, it is positioned as a managed SyMoNeuRaL assistant tied to the transportation workflow: bookings, quote logic, dispatch steps, reminders, and review requests.

That distinction is the whole of the difference. A general assistant knows nothing about this operation and can only answer in generalities. A managed assistant works against the operator's own service areas, policies, vehicle information and quote rules, inside a workflow that has actual states — booked, assigned, completed — rather than a chat window with no memory of what the business does.

The preview does not include source code, prompts, model files, deployment details, or final commercial terms. Production use requires written scope and approved SyMoNeuRaL-managed infrastructure.

Cloud-hosted first, local box later if needed

There are two ways to run this. Both are described so the choice is visible rather than made quietly on somebody's behalf.

Cloud-hosted Recommended first

  • SyMoNeuRaL runs the server.
  • The operator uses a protected web link.
  • Updates, backups and maintenance stay on SyMoNeuRaL's side.
  • Nothing to buy and nothing to maintain in order to try it.

Local box Later

  • The operator owns dedicated hardware.
  • SyMoNeuRaL can size, build and tune it.
  • Worth considering later for full control, heavier fleet usage, or less dependence on recurring hosting.
  • More up-front hardware and setup responsibility.
Why cloud first is the lighter path. It removes the largest up-front cost and the whole of the maintenance burden at exactly the moment when the thing being tested is whether the workflow is worth having at all. Buying hardware to find that out is spending money in the wrong order. Prove the loop first; a local box is an upgrade path afterwards, never a prerequisite for trying it.
